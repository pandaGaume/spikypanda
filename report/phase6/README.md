# Phase 6: RS-385 load ramp-up, second by second

This folder is the **observability study** of the RS-385 brushed-DC motor under a
single, slow load increase (a "montée en charge"). Instead of discrete operating
regimes, the motor runs at a constant drive voltage while ONE load torque ramps
from light to heavy; the resulting signals are then decomposed second by second
so every value AND every signal shape can be read off and explained from the
governing equations. Everything here is generated headless and physics-validated
before any plot is drawn.

## The experiment

- **Motor:** RS-385 brushed DC (R = 1.22 Ω, L = 1 mH, Kt = Ke = 8.22 mN·m/A, J = 6e-7 kg·m², b = 1.03e-6).
- **Drive:** constant **7 V**.
- **Load:** one Load Torque ramping **2 → 22 mN·m over 10 s**, then held (a single `ramp`-profile node).
- **Fault:** a rotor imbalance provides the 1x ("once per shaft revolution", i.e. at the rotation frequency) vibration.
- **Sampling:** the sim runs at 5 kHz (dt = 2e-4 s) for 12 s.

Because the electromechanical time constant τ_m ≈ 10 ms is tiny next to the 10 s
ramp, the motor is **quasi-steady** the whole time: at every instant it sits at
the closed-form steady point for the load it sees right then. The ramp therefore
sweeps the operating point so each quantity moves for a textbook reason, and the
simulation matches the closed form to **< 1 % every second**.

## What the data shows

Across the ramp (load 4 → 22 mN·m over the analysed window):

| quantity | start | end | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| speed ω | 765 | 446 rad/s | load brakes the rotor: ω = (Kt·V/R − τ) / (Kt·Ke/R + b) |
| current I | 0.58 | 2.73 A | more torque needed: I ≈ τ/Kt |
| 1x frequency | 122 | 71 Hz | f_mech = ω/2π |
| 1x vibration | 2.77 | 0.46 m/s² | centrifugal force ∝ ω², read as acceleration |

Two physics points the report develops in full:

1. **The 1x vibration falls FASTER than ω² (here ~ω^3.3), and it is NOT a
   resonance effect.** The imbalance force is ∝ ω², but an accelerometer reads
   *acceleration* = ω²·displacement, and the displacement itself tracks the force
   (∝ ω²) well below the 500 Hz housing mode, so the ideal limit is ω⁴. The
   measured ~ω^3.3 lands below 4 because of the discrete 4 kHz bracket integrator,
   not the (far-above-band) resonance.
2. **The first 0.5 s is skipped on purpose.** At t = 0 the full imbalance force
   hits a bracket at rest, so the acceleration overshoots to ≈ 30 m/s² and rings
   down at the bracket's ≈ 500 Hz natural mode (settling by ≈ 30 ms as simulated).
   That startup transient is unrelated to the load sweep, so the per-second
   decomposition starts after it has died out.

## Folder contents

```
phase6/
  phase6-rampup-analysis.md   the full second-by-second report (start here)
  phase6-signals.ipynb        Jupyter notebook: 8 matplotlib figures from the CSVs
  data/
    phase6-signals.csv        full f(t) series decimated to 1 kHz (t,tau,omega,current,accelY,accelZ)
    phase6-startup.csv         full-rate (5 kHz) housing accelY over the first 120 ms
  images/
    phase6-loadspeed.svg       load tau and speed omega (twin axis)
    phase6-current.svg         armature current
    phase6-vib-envelope.svg    1x vibration envelope across the ramp
    phase6-vib-zoom.svg        accY waveform, early vs late
    phase6-spectrum.svg        accY spectrum at three instants (1x peak sliding down)
    phase6-scaling.svg         measured 1x vs the omega^2 and omega^4 laws
    phase6-startup-transient.svg  the housing startup ring-down
  graphs/
    phase6-rampup.spikypanda  the whole montage as an editable node-editor graph
```

- [phase6-rampup-analysis.md](phase6-rampup-analysis.md) is the primary deliverable:
  the per-second table, a "value AND shape" section per signal, the chain of
  causation, and the startup-transient explanation. Its figures are the SVGs in
  `images/`.
- [phase6-signals.ipynb](phase6-signals.ipynb) reads the CSVs in `data/` and
  redraws every curve with matplotlib (needs only `numpy`, `pandas`, `matplotlib`;
  no scipy). Run it from this folder so the relative `data/` paths resolve.
- [graphs/phase6-rampup.spikypanda](graphs/phase6-rampup.spikypanda) reproduces the
  exact montage with real registry nodes (drive slider, DC motor, a `ramp`-profile
  Load Torque, the rotor imbalance wired to the motor's `fault_0`, housing, IMU,
  current sensor) plus line and FFT tiles. Open it in the node editor to play it.

## Reproducing

The whole folder is regenerated by the headless harness, which also asserts the
physics before writing anything:

- `packages/tests/privates/microg/rs385-rampup.test.ts` writes the report, the
  CSVs and the SVGs, and certifies (ω, I) vs the closed form, the montée-en-charge
  narrative, and the steeper-than-ω² vibration law.
- `packages/tests/motorwatch/phase6-rampup-graph.test.ts` generates the
  `.spikypanda` graph and verifies it loads and runs (motor slows under the ramp,
  imbalance vibration present).

The generator lives in `packages/dev/applications/privates/microg/src/rampup.report.ts`
(+ `rampup.plots.ts` for the SVGs).
